LIBRARY MANAGEMENT SYSTEM — REQUIREMENTS
========================================

1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
--------------------

The university currently coordinates borrowing requests, reservations, issue records,
and returns using registers, spreadsheets, and informal communication. The system
must demonstrate a reliable lending lifecycle for physical library resources, with
clear availability, review, and copy-state handling.

The target deliverable is a locally runnable prototype. The challenge permits
in-memory storage, local files, JSON, CSV, SQLite, or another lightweight local
approach. Python is preferred. Cloud deployment, paid services, external APIs,
complex infrastructure, and university production-system access are not required.
Authentication is optional.

2. STAKEHOLDERS
---------------

- Student / library member: search titles, check availability, submit and track
  requests, cancel an eligible reservation, and understand outcomes.
- Librarian / request reviewer: review requests, approve or reject, issue books,
  record returns, and manage copy restrictions.
- Library administrator: maintain catalogue, members, copies, and policies
  (administrator capabilities are inferred, not explicitly required).
- University/library management: may need operational and circulation reporting;
  specific reporting requirements are not stated.
- Evaluator/demo operator: run the prototype locally and observe end-to-end and
  conflict/failure scenarios.

3. FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
--------------------------

Catalogue and inventory

FR-01. Maintain book-title records with at least a title and category.
FR-02. Represent each title with individually identifiable physical copies.
       Availability must be evaluated per copy, not only from a title-level count.
FR-03. Track a copy's operational availability. Support unavailable states such as
       damaged, under repair, lost, reference-only, or another operational restriction.
FR-04. Show useful availability for a requested period and explain why no copy can
       satisfy the request.
FR-05. Provide the initial sample catalogue and statuses supplied in the challenge
       for demonstration and testing:
       - Clean Code — Software Engineering — 4 copies; 2 issued until 4 Aug 2026.
       - Introduction to Algorithms — Algorithms — 3 copies; 1 reserved from
         4 Aug 2026 to 8 Aug 2026.
       - Design Patterns — Software Design — 2 copies; 1 unavailable due to damage.
       - Database System Concepts — Databases — 5 copies; all available.
       - Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach — Artificial Intelligence —
         3 copies; 1 issued until 4 Aug 2026 and 1 reserved from 6 Aug 2026 to
         7 Aug 2026.

Borrowing requests and availability

FR-06. Record each borrowing request's member, selected title, borrowing start date,
       and expected return date.
FR-07. Validate member and title references and requested dates. A return date must
       be after the borrowing start date.
FR-08. Before submission, identify at least one operationally available copy that
       has no overlapping commitment for the full requested period.
FR-09. Do not commit a physical copy to overlapping active borrowing requests,
       confirmed reservations, or active loans.
FR-10. Prevent submission when no copy of the selected title is available for the
       requested period. Communicate the reason clearly and do not silently
       substitute another title.
FR-11. A successful submission enters a review process and retains its copy
       commitment while active, according to the selected policy.

Review and reservations

FR-12. An appropriate reviewer can inspect, approve, or reject a submitted request.
FR-13. Rejection closes the request and releases its associated copy commitment.
FR-14. Rejected requests cannot be revised in place; the requester must submit a
       new request with revised details.
FR-15. Approval results in a confirmed reservation or loan according to the agreed
       policy. The copy commitment must transfer without a gap or double allocation.
FR-16. An authorized end user can cancel a confirmed reservation before issue.
       Cancellation releases the copy for another valid request.

Issue and return

FR-17. Staff can issue a reserved copy and record an active loan.
FR-18. Staff can record a return and release the associated copy.
FR-19. During return, staff can mark the copy unavailable (for example, damaged or
       requiring repair). Such a copy must not be offered for future requests.
FR-20. Keep request, reservation, loan, and copy states consistent through approval,
       rejection, cancellation, issue, and return.

User feedback and demonstration

FR-21. Present actionable validation, conflict, and operation-failure messages.
FR-22. Demonstrate at least one end-to-end lending flow and one conflict, invalid,
       or failure scenario.
FR-23. Show current request status and relevant title/copy availability to users
       who need the information.

4. NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
------------------------------

NFR-01. Local deployability: Run locally and provide prerequisites, setup, and
        startup instructions. No cloud deployment, paid service, external API,
        complex infrastructure, or production-system access is needed.
NFR-02. Persistence: Use an allowed lightweight local storage approach. Document
        the choice, restart/data-loss behavior, and sample/reset process.
NFR-03. Consistency: Prevent overlapping copy commitments and keep lifecycle and
        copy state consistent, including during competing submissions and state
        transitions.
NFR-04. Usability: Make common member and staff tasks understandable with clear
        status labels, validation, and explanations for blocked requests.
NFR-05. Reliability: Invalid or conflicting operations must fail visibly and safely;
        they must not double-book a copy or leave an unintended commitment.
NFR-06. Maintainability and explainability: Keep the prototype small and structured
        enough for the team to explain its rules, validation, and design choices.
        Document trade-offs and known limitations.
NFR-07. Security and privacy: Authentication is optional for the challenge. If
        omitted, disclose that the prototype is not suitable for real member data
        or public deployment. Avoid collecting unnecessary personal information.
NFR-08. Testability: Validate core rules with repeatable tests or scenarios,
        including overlap conflicts, rejection release, reservation cancellation,
        issue/return, and return with an unavailable status.
NFR-09. Accessibility and compatibility: Use readable labels and feedback and
        support the agreed local browser/device environment. Specific standards
        require customer confirmation.
NFR-10. Performance and scale: No production scale or response-time targets are
         stated. Remain responsive for the supplied demonstration dataset; confirm
         production targets with the customer.

5. BUSINESS RULES AND WORKFLOW
------------------------------

- Availability is determined per physical copy, for the full requested period.
- A candidate copy must be operationally available and have no overlapping active
  request, confirmed reservation, or active loan.
- If no copy satisfies the request, submission is blocked with a clear reason.
- A submitted request must be reviewed before becoming a confirmed reservation or
  loan.
- Rejection closes the request and releases its copy. Revised information requires
  a new request.
- A confirmed reservation may be cancelled before issue; cancellation releases its
  copy.
- A return releases its copy unless staff marks it unavailable during return.
- Any operational restriction prevents the copy from being allocated until cleared.
- Approval, rejection, cancellation, issue, and return must not introduce an
  unintended gap, double-booking, or stranded copy commitment.
- Partial fulfilment and automatic alternative-book recommendations are not
  required.

6. SUGGESTED ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
-------------------------------

AC-01. Accept a valid request only if at least one copy can cover its requested
       period.
AC-02. Block an overlapping request against the only eligible copy and explain why.
AC-03. Reject missing/unknown member or title data, invalid dates, and a return
       date that is not after the start date.
AC-04. After rejection, close the request and allow a later valid request to use
       the released copy.
AC-05. Allow cancellation of a confirmed, not-yet-issued reservation and release
       its copy; refuse cancellation after issue.
AC-06. Issuing a reservation creates an active loan that blocks overlapping
       requests.
AC-07. Returning a loan releases the copy unless it is marked unavailable.
AC-08. Prevent allocation of damaged, under-repair, lost, reference-only, or
       otherwise unavailable copies.
AC-09. Preserve consistent commitments across approval, rejection, cancellation,
       issue, and return.
AC-10. Documented instructions start the app locally, and the demo can show a
       successful lifecycle plus a conflict/failure scenario.

7. PROTOTYPE SCOPE AND EXCLUSIONS
---------------------------------

Recommended priorities for the 90-minute challenge:

1. Model titles, individual copies, members, requests, reservations, and loans.
2. Implement date-aware availability and conflict prevention.
3. Implement review, rejection/release, cancellation, issue, and return, including
   marking a copy unavailable on return.
4. Provide a simple local UI, sample data, clear messages, and repeatable checks.
5. Document setup, assumptions, exclusions, trade-offs, and known limitations.

Explicitly not required:

- Partial fulfilment or automatic recommendations for alternative titles.
- Cloud deployment, paid services, complex infrastructure, external APIs, or
  university production integrations.
- Authentication, although a team may implement it and explain why it was
  prioritized.

Likely additional prototype exclusions, subject to customer confirmation:

- Fines, payments, overdue reminders, renewals, analytics, branch transfers,
  production identity integration, and production-grade role/permission management.
- High availability, multi-instance scaling, formal disaster recovery, and
  compliance certification.

8. RISKS AND DESIGN CONSIDERATIONS
----------------------------------

- A lightweight local datastore is quick to build but may not safely support
  multiple concurrent application instances.
- Without authentication, users may impersonate members or staff. Do not use real
  personal data or expose the prototype publicly.
- A pending request that reserves a copy can block other users if review is delayed;
  production operations need a timeout or queue policy.
- The supplied activity dates are in August 2026. As of 14 October 2026 they are
  historical, so the demo must clarify whether to retain them as history or rebase
  them.
- The challenge does not define approval-versus-issue behavior or date-boundary
  semantics; inconsistent choices can cause double-booking or false conflicts.
